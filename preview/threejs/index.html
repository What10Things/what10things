What10Things · Design experiment

Ten things,
held in orbit

This page is an experiment. It takes a single eight-second sequence generated with Google Flow (Veo) — an amber core ringed by ten orbiting points — and turns it into a scroll-driven knowledge surface. Scrolling does not play a video: it steps through the generated frames one by one, forwards and backwards, on a Three.js texture.

Scroll — the generated Flow frames advance with you ↓

01

One generated sequence, treated as a timeline

The Flow clip was decoded once into ninety-six still frames. Those frames are the only visual source on this page. As you scroll down, later frames are drawn onto a canvas and shown on a flat plane in 3D; scroll up and the earlier frames return. The motion you see is the original render, scrubbed by scroll position rather than by a play head.

02

Why a knowledge surface

What10Things guides are built around ten distinct points. The generated sequence happens to show ten lit nodes circling a centre, so it reads as a natural stand-in for a single guide: one subject, ten things worth knowing, each holding its own position.

03

The 3D layer is deliberately quiet

Around the footage there is a small amount of genuine three-dimensional treatment: the camera drifts through the composition as the chapters advance, and a handful of light nodes and relationship lines sit in front of the plane with real depth and parallax. The generated footage stays dominant; nothing is layered over its centre.

04

Built for a 390 px phone first

The scroll interaction runs from the same canvas texture on every device, including mobile Safari — it never depends on seeking a video element. Controls are at least 44 px, there is no hover requirement, and the layout holds without horizontal scrolling on a narrow portrait screen.

05

What this is not

This is not a production feature and not a claim about finished functionality. It is one generated sequence, a scroll mapping, and a restrained spatial frame around it — a way to feel out whether a guide could be read as a surface you move through rather than a page you scroll past.

06

Check the source for yourself

The control in the corner opens the exact Flow clip these frames came from, so the generated sequence and the scrubbed frames can be compared directly. The clip is only loaded if you open it.
